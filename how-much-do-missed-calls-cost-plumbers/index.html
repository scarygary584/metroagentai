Missed-call guide for plumbers

How much do missed calls cost plumbers?

A missed plumbing call is not automatically lost revenue, but it can be a real opportunity cost when the caller has a leak, backup, clogged drain, no hot water, or another urgent problem.

Use conservative numbers. This guide and calculator are planning tools only and do not guarantee booked jobs, revenue, or call volume.

The simple formula

Start with missed calls, booking rate, and job value

For a rough planning estimate, a plumbing company can multiply missed calls by the percentage of calls that might have booked and then multiply that by an average job value. Emergency or after-hours calls can be modeled separately if they tend to be higher-value jobs.

The point is not to pretend every missed caller becomes money. The point is to see whether better call answering could be worth fixing.

Why plumbing is different

Urgent callers may not wait

When someone has water on the floor, a sewer smell, a backed-up toilet, or a leaking water heater, they may call several companies quickly. If the first call goes unanswered, the customer can move on before a callback happens.

Emergency leaks

A leak call can be time-sensitive because the customer wants damage stopped quickly.

Sewer backups

Backup calls can be stressful and often lead to fast dialing behavior.

No hot water

Water heater calls may turn into booked service when the caller reaches someone quickly.

After-hours calls

Evening and weekend calls can be worth tracking separately from normal daytime calls.
